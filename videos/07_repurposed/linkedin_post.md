# LinkedIn Authority Post

In 2024, Google reported that ~25% of all new code was AI-generated.
By early 2026, Sundar Pichai stated that number reached 75%.

As an ex-Google engineer who worked across Gmail and Cloud, I sat down and mapped out my old weekly engineering routine into three columns:
1. Gone (Automated completely)
2. Helps (Speeds up, but requires human guardrails)
3. Worth MORE (The skills that became 5x more valuable)

Here is the blunt breakdown:

→ Boilerplate & Unit Tests: GONE.
Writing 40 lines of boilerplate to verify happy paths is a solved problem. Models test edge cases you would skip at 6 PM.

→ Code Reviews & Refactoring: HELPS.
AI catches lint violations and syntax bugs, but it has zero intuition about your company's legacy architecture or why that hack was deployed two quarters ago.

→ Systems Architecture & Verification: WORTH MORE.
When code generation cost collapses to zero, the value migrates to taste, specification, and verification. If you cannot specify what you want with mathematical precision, you get 10,000 lines of fast garbage.

The real moat isn't typing speed. It's knowing what to build and proving it actually works.
